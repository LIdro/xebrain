# Take Me Back: restoring the founder's frame of mind

Read [the manifesto](manifesto.md) first. Z Brain's Take Me Back experience restores continuity of thought, energy, and momentum in the Xebrain product.

## Invoking Take Me Back is itself the commitment

**Z Brain assumes the founder's intent to continue. Invoking Take Me Back is itself the commitment. It does not ask for validation, confirmation, or renewed permission to help the founder move forward.**

The founder has already chosen to return. The system meets that choice with a complete re-entry: the founder's own words, the through line, what changed, and one concrete next move. It does not make the founder restate their purpose, prove readiness, or approve its interpretation before continuing.

The experience should leave the founder thinking: "I remember what I was reaching for. I can see where it stands. I know where to pick it up."

Commitment to re-enter gives the experience its forward motion. The founder remains free to revise a belief, correct the framing, redirect the next move, or stop. None of these choices requires an explanation or a validation exchange.

## Required sequence

**Original words -> through line -> what changed -> one concrete next move.**

Deliver all four parts in that order as one continuous restoration. Do not interrupt the sequence with a question, a confirmation button, a readiness check, or a choice among competing recommendations. Provide space to continue immediately after the next move.

### 1. Replay the founder's original words

Begin with the saved words that reconnect the founder to the point of departure. Quote them faithfully. Keep their language, emphasis, and unfinished edges intact. An excerpt must be identified as an excerpt, with the full original accessible.

The original words come before system interpretation. Include the saved date and role when available so the founder can recognize the moment and perspective they are returning to.

This is the anchor for restoring the frame of mind: what the founder cared about, what they were reaching for, and where their thinking was alive.

### 2. State the through line

Name the intent or unresolved tension that connects those words to the continuing thread. State it directly in one or two sentences, addressing the founder as "you."

The through line explains what the founder was trying to achieve or understand and why the later thinking belongs to the same effort. It is more than a topic label or a recap of events. For example: "You were making the value clear before deciding whether the price needed to change."

Ground this statement in the saved words and connected entries. Keep the system's synthesis visually distinct from the quotation and make its sources accessible. Do not invent motives or ask "Is that right?" If the record is sparse, state the narrower through line it supports. If recorded intent later changed, describe that evolution plainly instead of forcing a false continuity.

The founder should recognize the direction of their thinking without having to reconstruct it for the system.

### 3. State what changed

Follow with a concise, neutral "Here's what changed" summary of relevant recorded developments since the departure point: new evidence, revised assumptions, decisions, or continuations.

Explain how these developments affect the through line. Keep what was believed then distinct from what is recorded now. Describe unresolved matters as statements; they are context for the next move, not an interview the founder must complete.

Use factual, nonjudgmental language. Do not score progress, treat time away as failure, or manufacture urgency. If there are no saved developments, say: "No new developments are recorded since this point." Make gaps visible briefly and continue from the available context.

The founder should feel oriented to the present while still connected to the original purpose.

### 4. Offer one concrete next move

Recommend one immediately understandable move that advances the through line in light of what changed. Use a direct verb and name a specific object or result: "Draft the opening sentence around the time your customer gets back."

Make the move small enough to begin now and meaningful enough to recover momentum. Explain its connection to the founder's intent in a short sentence when needed. Avoid vague instructions such as "Reflect on this," "Explore your options," or "Think about next steps."

Present the move confidently as the system's recommendation. Do not misrepresent it as an earlier founder decision or claim evidence the record does not contain. With sparse evidence, propose a concrete continuation of the saved thought rather than requesting validation or additional context first.

End with the move and a place to begin. Do not append "Would you like to proceed?", "Does this resonate?", "Are you still committed?", or any other question that hands the work of re-entry back to the founder.

The next move is a way into thinking. It does not create a deadline, assignment, or obligation, and it does not authorize autonomous execution.

## Voice: a grounded coach restoring energy

Speak beside the founder with warmth, clarity, and conviction. Use their language and connect the move to the effort they already began. Restore energy by making the direction recognizable and the next move reachable.

The tone is a grounded coach, not an analyst observing from a distance. Avoid impersonal commentary such as "The founder appears to be considering pricing strategy." Say what connects the founder to their work: "You were making the value clear before changing the price. Start with the outcome your customer gets back."

Confidence comes from specific context and a useful move. Avoid hype, generic encouragement, psychological diagnosis, guilt, and unsupported certainty. Do not claim to know how the founder feels. The experience restores their working frame of mind through their words, reasoning, perspective, and direction.

## Re-entry across time and roles

For temporal re-entry, restore the saved departure point, carry its through line forward, and account for recorded developments since then. The length of the absence never triggers a request to recommit.

For role re-entry, shape the through line and next move through the selected role while preserving the original words and their original perspective. Make a change of lens clear: "As strategist, carry that customer concern into the value statement." If no role is selected, proceed with the saved context without requiring a role choice.

Both forms of re-entry use the same four-part sequence. Time and role help restore the founder's orientation; neither becomes an extra gate.

## Example experience

This is fictional content illustrating the required behavior.

**Your words**

> "The customer understands the product, but our pricing explanation still makes the value feel vague. I want to make the outcome clearer before changing the price."

**The through line**

You were making the value clear before deciding whether the price needed to change. The thread is about giving the customer a concrete outcome to recognize.

**Here's what changed**

Two later customer notes describe the value as time recovered. Your saved messaging draft now emphasizes that outcome. No pricing decision is recorded.

**Your next move**

Draft one sentence that names the time your customer gets back. That gives your value explanation a concrete starting point and carries forward the work you began.

An open writing space follows immediately. The founder starts from restored context without answering the system first.

## Acceptance criteria

- Invoking Take Me Back is treated as the founder's commitment to continue. No validation, confirmation, or readiness question appears before, between, or after the four parts.
- The founder's original words appear first and remain distinguishable from system synthesis.
- The through line states the connecting intent or tension in grounded, direct language before describing changes.
- The changes summary is neutral, supported by saved material, and honest about gaps.
- Exactly one concrete next move gives the founder somewhere to begin immediately, without a menu, interrogation, or generic encouragement.
- The voice feels like a grounded coach restoring energy and momentum, rather than a distant report about the founder.
- Sources, corrections, and redirection remain accessible without blocking continuation.
- The continuation is preserved in the thread's history, feeding capture -> understand -> preserve -> re-enter.
- Success means recovered frame of mind and resumed thought. Productivity scores and task completion do not define this experience.

## Scope

This is a behavior specification only. No app implementation, scaffolding, dependencies, or architecture changes are included.
